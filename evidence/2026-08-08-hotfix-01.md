## H1 — the refusal was RIGHT; the stated cause was FALSE

`quotes.toscrape.com` — 214 pages fetched, 3,978 internal links, every page HTTP 200, 213 of 214
classified `content` by the kind classifier (`pages` stores no text length, so this is the classifier's
verdict rather than a re-derivable character count) — was refused as **`site_too_small_to_measure`** on the deployed
function. `coverage.excluded` was pagination 152 / archive 60 / auth 1, leaving `gradeable: 1`.

Withholding the letter is correct: one content page cannot support a link-structure measurement.
Telling a 214-page site it is *too small* is not. **Our own classifier cut the population and the
trigger reported the cut as a property of the site** — the invented-cause class this spec exists to
delete, reached through the coverage accounting rather than the render path.

### The split — categorical, over a population the exclusions can account for

`decideRefusal` distinguishes three truths under one refusal. The only number is the floor that
already existed:

| condition | trigger | the truth it states |
|---|---|---|
| crawl truncated, or truncation unknown | `too_few_gradeable_pages` | we may not have reached enough of a larger site |
| completed, `gradeable + Σexcluded < floor` | `site_too_small_to_measure` | we hold the whole site and it is genuinely small |
| completed, `gradeable + Σexcluded >= floor` | **`too_few_gradeable_after_exclusion`** | the site had the pages; **we** excluded them |

> ⚠ **THE FIRST ATTEMPT USED `coverage.fetched` AND THAT WAS A SHIPPED DEFECT.** `fetched` is "every
> URL fetched, ANY status" and includes non-200 and off-host pages; `coverage.excluded` is tallied only
> over same-host-200 pages. Two populations, so the printed numbers could not reconcile — and a
> reviewer measured the consequence: a genuine 3-page brochure with 10 broken links reads
> `fetched 13 / gradeable 3 / excluded []` and was routed to the exclusion branch, **told its pages
> were excluded when none were**. That regressed the exact case the hotfix exists to preserve, into the
> invented-cause class it exists to delete. The wedge is live in production: 4 of the 15 rows carrying
> coverage have `fetched > gradeable + Σexcluded` (freepltn is **24** unaccounted).
>
> The population is now `gradeable + Σexcluded`. Two properties follow **by construction** rather than
> from a second guard: the exclusion branch implies `Σexcluded > 0`, and the shortfall the copy
> narrates **is** `Σexcluded`. Non-200, off-host and failed fetches never enter the decision.

`excludedPageCount === null` falls back to the small-site branch — asserting "we excluded most of your
pages" without holding the tally would be the same invented cause in a new hat.

## H2 — the fingerprint is threaded, AND sanitized at the persist boundary

**0 of 231 production audits carried one** (the 0 is the load-bearing half; the denominator has since
drifted to 245 rows / 229 completed — see §10's drift note). `crawler.ts:832` built it and set
`out.fingerprint`; `analyzeCrawl` never read it and `AuditResult` had no field for it, so
`persist-results.ts:182`'s guard was always false.

**Why nothing caught it:** `fingerprintFor`, `boundFingerprintForPersist` and the persist branch are
each tested in isolation, and the persist tests supply a fingerprint directly. The missing propagation
sat in the gap **between** two well-tested halves — the same shape as `AuditView.tsx` being in no test.

> ⚠ **THREADING IT MADE A CRAWL-DESTROYING DEFECT REACHABLE, AND A REVIEWER CAUGHT IT BEFORE MERGE.**
> `templateKeyFor` percent-DECODES each path segment, so an ordinary anchor on any page we crawl —
> `<a href="/%00section/some-slug">` — yields a stratum key containing a **raw NUL**. Verified:
>
> ```
> templateKeyFor('https://example.com/%00section/some-slug-here')
>   → "/\u0000section/{slug}"        HAS_RAW_NUL: true
> ```
>
> Postgres rejects that in `jsonb` (`unsupported Unicode escape sequence`), the completion `update`
> throws, and `onFailure` marks the audit **failed** — a crawl that succeeded is destroyed, from one
> link, with no cooperation from the crawled server. It was unreachable only because the fingerprint
> was being dropped; this change is what would have made it live.

**Sanitized at the PERSIST BOUNDARY ONLY.** `boundFingerprintForPersist` maps every crawled string in
the fingerprint through `toPersistableText` — *"THE one call every crawled string bound for a database
column must go through"*. `templateKeyFor` is **unchanged and must stay so**: the in-memory key is the
SELECTION IDENTITY that decides which URLs are sampled, so normalising it at the source would move
grades to fix a storage problem.

The same bound closes a second reviewer finding: keys are capped at 256 bytes. Intermediate segments
stay literal, so one 1,500-character segment produced a 1,508-character key and 100 strata could reach
**~200 KB** in a single row.

> ⚠ **THE POST-FIX WORST CASE IS ~57 kB, NOT THE "~6.5 kB worst case, bounded" THE MIGRATION NOTE
> DOCUMENTS — and this file quoted that figure as the thing being violated while leaving it standing.**
> The budget counts UTF-8 bytes; JSON escaping DOUBLES `"` and `\`, so a 256-byte key of quotes
> serialises to 513.
>
> **THE TWO FIGURES ARE TWO DIFFERENT PAYLOADS, not one row** — an earlier wording presented them as a
> single measurement, and the exact digits it quoted (`57,579` / `34,618`) are not re-derivable. At 100
> bounded strata a reviewer measured: quote-maximal keys → ~57 kB on the wire but ~1 kB on disk (pglz
> compresses them); high-entropy keys → ~34 kB on disk but ~31 kB on the wire. Each figure is the worst
> case for its own dimension, and no single payload is worst at both. The query, so a reader can
> re-derive rather than trust:
>
> ```sql
> select octet_length(v::text), pg_column_size(v) from (
>   select jsonb_build_object('version', 1, 'strata', (
>     select jsonb_agg(jsonb_build_object('templateKey', repeat('"', 256), 'discovered', 1, 'selected', 1))
>     from generate_series(1, 100))) as v
> ) t;   -- 56,661 | 33,816
> ```
>
> Reachable — `templateKeyFor` percent-decodes, and 256 `"` cost 768 URL characters, well inside
> `MAX_URL_LENGTH` (2048).
>
> **The BEHAVIOUR is deliberately unchanged** (owner ruling): 57 kB is negligible beside the already
> bounded `ai_readiness` (1.90 MB pre-bound), so budgeting on JSON-escaped bytes would buy nothing. What
> was defective was the NUMBER. It is now stated where the cap is defined, and here.

The fixture drives a crawled `%00` through the real producer into **real Postgres**, with an
anti-vacuity case proving the unsanitized key is genuinely rejected, and varies the control character
rather than testing one instance (§10's matcher-class rule).

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| revert the propagation | `3 failed \| 849 passed (852)` (full `packages/engine` suite) |
| revert the deep walk to the old three-field allowlist | `1 failed \| 10 passed (11)` — *THE WALK IS STRUCTURAL* |
| remove the self-declaring truncation tag | `1 failed \| 10 passed (11)` — *A TRUNCATED KEY SAYS SO* |

*"`proxy_gradeable < 5` can only happen when `page_count < 5`"* was withdrawn in

Corrected. And because correcting-the-file-in-hand-while-asserting-globally is now the **second**

`apps/web/__tests__/docs-withdrawn-claims.test.ts` holds a registry of withdrawn propositions and

meaning, so a paraphrase passes; inside an `allowedIn` file it cannot distinguish quoting-to-withdraw

`*.md` only. Allowances are themselves checked — an allowance for a claim its file no longer contains
fails the suite, which is how the first draft's bogus allowance was caught.

### What else changed

- **The recursive string walk.** `boundFingerprintForPersist` no longer maps three named fields; it
  rebuilds the object from `Object.entries` and sanitizes every string leaf, so *"every crawled
  string"* is true by construction. The reviewer's proof — adding one string field to the fingerprint
  and one to a stratum, both reaching Postgres raw — is now a test that adds fields which deliberately
  do not exist in `CrawlFingerprint`, because the failure mode is the field added tomorrow.
- **A truncated key says so, and uniquely.** `/aaa…(1500)/{slug}` and `/aaa…(1500)x/{slug}` persisted
  as the same 256-byte key: two sections of a site sharing a name, in the artifact whose job is naming
  which section moved, with nothing recording that a cut happened (§10). A cut string now carries `~`
  plus 8 hex of the sha256 of its full sanitized value — self-declaring, collision-resistant,
  deterministic across crawls, and absent when no cut occurred.
- **The prototype guard is real.** `excludedLabel` checks `Object.hasOwn` before the lookup and

### The three code fixes

**Keys are sanitized, not only values.** Two reviewers found this independently: `Object.entries`
yields `[k, v]` and only `v` was walked, so `{"byTemplate": {"/a\0b": 3}}` reached Postgres raw and
threw the original crash verbatim. `Record<templateKey, count>` is the obvious shape for this artifact,
so it was one refactor away. A caveat four lines below the word EVERY does not make EVERY true.

**An audit can no longer fail for fingerprint bookkeeping.** `safeFingerprintForPersist` wraps the
bound: on any throw the audit persists with `fingerprint` null, keeps its grade, pages, links and
findings, and the drop is logged. This is the NUL lesson as a structural property rather than one more
cause closed on its merits — closing causes one at a time is what produced three gates of "the class,
one radius smaller". Losing the fingerprint is cheap and queryable; losing the audit is not. No depth
or cycle machinery was added: the producer is our own two-level constructor, and that limit is stated
beside the guard rather than defended against speculatively.

**The tag applies to any lossy transform, and hashes the RAW input.** It was applied only on the length
path, so a control-strip — the original `%00` vector — silently merged two distinct sections:
`"/\0section/{slug}"` and `"/section/{slug}"` persisted as one name, untagged, 1 distinct key of 2. The
docstring called the tag "collision-resistant between distinct originals", true of the length path
only. Hashing the raw string makes it true of both.

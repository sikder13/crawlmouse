## H1 — the refusal was RIGHT; the stated cause was FALSE

`quotes.toscrape.com` — 214 pages fetched, 3,978 internal links, every page HTTP 200, 213 of 214
carrying ≥ 80 characters of main text — was refused as **`site_too_small_to_measure`** on the deployed
function. `coverage.excluded` was pagination 152 / archive 60 / auth 1, leaving `gradeable: 1`.

Withholding the letter is correct: one content page cannot support a link-structure measurement.
Telling a 214-page site it is *too small* is not. **Our own classifier cut the population and the
trigger reported the cut as a property of the site** — the invented-cause class this spec exists to
delete, reached through the coverage accounting rather than the render path.

### The split — categorical, no new threshold

`decideRefusal` now distinguishes three truths under one refusal. The only number involved is the
floor that already existed:

| condition | trigger | the truth it states |
|---|---|---|
| crawl truncated, or truncation unknown | `too_few_gradeable_pages` | we may not have reached enough of a larger site |
| completed, `fetched < MIN_GRADEABLE_PAGES` | `site_too_small_to_measure` | we hold the whole site and it is genuinely small |
| completed, `fetched >= MIN_GRADEABLE_PAGES` | **`too_few_gradeable_after_exclusion`** | the site had the pages; **we** excluded them |

`fetchedPageCount === null` falls back to the small-site branch, never the new one — asserting "we
excluded most of your pages" without holding the count would be the same invented cause in a new hat.

The evidence gained one field, `fetchedPageCount`, fed from `coverage.fetched` so **the gate and the
copy quote the same number**.

### The copy names our composition, never the site

Through the single `NO_GRADE_EXPLANATION` seam in `lib/refusal-copy.ts`, checked **before** the
small-site branch. It renders, for the production shape:

> **Too few content pages to grade the link structure**
> We crawled 214 pages of this site, and most of them aren’t content we grade: 152 pagination pages,
> 60 tag or category archives, 1 login or account pages.
> That left 1 content page — too few to measure how a site links itself together.
> Tag, category, archive and pagination pages are how a site is organised, not what it is about, so we
> don’t grade them. Below 5 content pages we don’t publish a letter. This is about what we can measure
> — it isn’t a judgement about the size or quality of your site.

- Composition comes from `coverage.excluded`, biggest first, **bounded at three kinds and it says so**
  when bounded ("and N other kinds") — §10, no silent truncation.
- The floor is read from `MIN_GRADEABLE_PAGES`, never a literal.
- **No upsell.** A larger crawl budget cannot change a classification decision, so suggesting one
  would be a second falsehood. `next` is `null`.
- Degrades honestly with no accounting: it invents no composition and still never says "too small".

**(B) The historical corpus, and why it cannot be replayed.** 214 completed audits predate the
coverage column, so the kind classifier cannot be re-run over them. Using the same proxy §5's sign-off
used (`status_code = 200 ∧ NOT excluded_from_grade`):

```
historical_completed                     214
below_floor_proxy                         40
would_be_new_trigger (lower bound)         0     ← the proxy CANNOT see this shape
genuinely_small (lower bound)             27
```

**The proxy returns 0 for the very shape that dominates the live data.** It is blind by construction:
it omits precisely the kind exclusions at issue, so `proxy_gradeable < 5` can only happen when
`page_count < 5` — i.e. it can only ever report "genuinely small".

### This corrects §5's caveat with data

`OPERATING-RULES` §5 signs off against **51 of 215 (23.7%)**, computed on that proxy, of which **27**
are attributed to `site_too_small_to_measure`.
`evidence/2026-08-04-stage4-floor-calibration.md` already called the total a **lower bound**
(*"applying the missing exclusions can only move audits into refusal, never out"*). What the live data
adds is that **the composition was wrong too, not just the total**: on real coverage, 5 of 6
small-site refusals are the exclusion shape. The 27 is therefore best read as *"below the floor by the
proxy"*, not as *"27 genuinely small sites"* — and after this hotfix most of that population would
carry the new trigger instead.

§5 is annotated accordingly. A representative re-measurement needs coverage on a real corpus and is
left as scheduled work rather than guessed at here.

## H2 — the fingerprint is threaded

**0 of 231 production audits carried one.** `crawler.ts:832` built it and set `out.fingerprint`;
`analyzeCrawl` never read it and `AuditResult` had no field for it, so
`persist-results.ts:182`'s `result.fingerprint ? … : {}` was always false.

Fixed by declaring `AuditResult.fingerprint` and carrying `crawlOut.fingerprint` through
`analyzeCrawl`. The persist guard and `boundFingerprintForPersist` already existed and are untouched.

**Why nothing caught it:** `fingerprintFor` is tested in isolation, `boundFingerprintForPersist` is
tested in isolation, and the persist tests supply a fingerprint on the input object directly. The
missing propagation sat in the gap **between** two well-tested halves — the same shape as
`AuditView.tsx` being in no test at all. The new tests assert the seam itself.

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| revert the propagation | `3 failed \| 29 passed (32)` |

**Acceptance is post-deploy and is NOT met yet:** one real production audit must carry a persisted
fingerprint, verified via MCP. That is the closing condition, exactly as B17 was.

## H3 — full-history secret scan: CLEAN, nothing to rotate

The repository is public (`private: false`, verified). Two independent scans:

| scan | scope | result |
|---|---|---|
| gitleaks 8.28.0, `git --log-opts=--all` | 609 commits, 8.18 MB | **no leaks found** |
| exhaustive blob sweep | **5,411 distinct blobs** reachable from every ref | **0 matches** |

The blob sweep exists because the two commit counts did not agree — gitleaks reported 609 where the
repo has 629 reachable commits (611 non-merge). Rather than explain the gap, it was made irrelevant:
every version of every file ever committed is a blob, so walking blobs cannot skip a merge commit or a
traversal edge case. Patterns: `sk-`, `sk_live_`, `rk_live_`, `whsec_`, `ghp_`, `gho_`, `github_pat_`,
AWS `AKIA`/`ASIA`, `AIza`, PEM private keys, JWT `eyJhbGciOi`, `re_`, `phc_`, `xox[baprs]-`, SendGrid.

`PROJECT_OVERVIEW` §9 said "(private)" and now says PUBLIC, with the scan result and the practical
consequence recorded beside it.
